# Cluster headache: an open evidence base

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.24233067.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24233067)

A machine-readable, reproducible record of what is known about cluster headache — every registered trial and its result, the eight genetic risk loci and what they do, the mechanism findings, and who is actually researching and funding the disease. Plus the code that regenerates all of it from public sources.

Cluster headache affects roughly 1 in 1,000 people and is among the most painful conditions in medicine. It has, at the time of writing, **one NIH-funded research lab in the United States**. The literature is scattered and no maintained synthesis exists. This repository is an attempt at one.

## Two different things live here, and they are kept apart

**`docs/evidence-log.md` — the record.** Trials, results, effect sizes, genetic loci, provocation studies, funding. Sourced and dated. This is the part intended to be useful to anyone working on cluster headache, whatever they think of the ideas below.

**`docs/hypotheses.md` — the claims.** Eleven working hypotheses generated by analysing the record, including one about the 5-HT7 receptor that is novel as far as the literature shows. These are hypotheses. They are ranked, their unresolved objections are stated alongside them, and none has been tested.

If you only want one of those, take the record.

## What the analyses found

Eight computational analyses, all reproducible from `scripts/`. The substantive results:

- **Cluster headache risk genes are enriched in artery tissue, not brain.** Of 49 GTEx tissues, tibial artery, coronary artery and aorta survive multiple-testing correction; hypothalamus does not (p = 0.91). 13 of 16 risk genes are elevated in artery.
- **Risk alleles change gene expression mainly in arteries.** LRP1 up in tibial artery (p = 9e-21), UFL1 up, PLCE1 down. No hypothalamic eQTL at any locus.
- **A two-hit model follows:** attacks appear to need both an inherited vulnerable vessel wall and an open hypothalamic timing window. Neither alone is sufficient.
- **The drugs that end a cluster bout all block the 5-HT7 receptor.** Methysergide, dihydroergotamine, ergotamine and BOL-148 are antagonists. Frovatriptan, which has no lasting effect, is a full agonist. 5-HT7 potency separates bout-enders from attack-stoppers by about elevenfold.
- **HTR7 is the only candidate specific to both halves of the two-hit model.** Of 514 background genes, none scores higher on joint artery-and-hypothalamus tissue specificity. No cluster headache risk gene is dual; they are artery-only.
- **No paper has ever paired 5-HT7 with cluster headache.** Zero results in PubMed.
- **Funding is close to absent.** In NIH's entire grant database, exactly one currently funded project has cluster headache in its title.

Two objections to the 5-HT7 lead are unresolved and stated in full in `docs/hypotheses.md`: 5-HT2A cannot be separated from 5-HT7 on the available data, and prejunctional 5-HT7 activation *inhibits* CGRP release, so blockade may lift a brake on the pathway that galcanezumab targets. Both are experiments, not arguments.

## Reproducing it

Everything is read-only against public APIs. No credentials, no private data, no patient data of any kind.

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
mkdir -p data
python3 scripts/<script>.py
```

Sources: ClinicalTrials.gov API v2, NIH RePORTER, GTEx v8, PubChem (which carries the IUPHAR/Guide to Pharmacology curated ligand tables), Open Targets, PubMed E-utilities, OpenAIRE.

| Analysis | Scripts |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Trial landscape | `fetch_trials.py`, `classify_trials.py` |
| 2. Risk-gene tissue enrichment | `gtex.py`, `gtex_bg.py`, `tissue_enrich.py` |
| 3. Receptor signature | `chembl_profiles.py`, `signature.py`, `screen_5ht7.py`, `pubmed_counts.py` |
| 4. Two-hit model | synthesis; no script |
| 5. Risk-variant eQTLs | `eqtl_lookup.py` |
| 6. 5-HT7 functional direction | `ht7_*.py`, `dual_profile.py` |
| 7. Who researches cluster headache | `who_runs_trials.py`, `who_publishes.py` |
| 8. Who funds it | `nih_grants.py`, `nih_grants2.py`, `grants_wide.py`, `industry.py` |

`docs/analyses.md` has the methods and the limitations of each.

## Known limitations

Stated here rather than buried, because they bound what the record can support.

- GTEx contains no trigeminal ganglion, no sphenopalatine ganglion and no cranial arteries. "Artery" here means tibial, aorta and coronary — peripheral proxies for the vessels that matter.
- The Winsvold 2023 GWAS summary statistics are not public, so the genetic work uses lead variants only and Mendelian randomisation is not currently possible.
- Many of the receptor direction labels in the curated pharmacology databases date from the binding era and rest on older functional assays. Psilocin's direction at 5-HT7 is unknown.
- The drug-comparison statistics rest on six compounds. With six, the lowest attainable p-value is 0.067. The separation is as clean as six drugs permit and no cleaner.
- Trial coverage is registry-based. Unregistered and non-English trials are missed.
- The grant analysis is NIH-centred. European national funders, private foundations and industry are only partly captured, and a UK query failed outright.

## Provenance

The analyses were produced by a cluster headache patient working with AI assistance, against public data. Everything is reproducible from the scripts here; nothing rests on a claim you have to take on trust. Errors are mine — please open an issue.

## Contributing

Corrections to the record are the most valuable contribution: a missed trial, a wrong effect size, a superseded result. Open an issue or a pull request.

If you research cluster headache and something here is wrong, I would rather know than be right.
